## 1 · Rewritten strings (BEFORE → AFTER)

### `1ch-21-17` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
David's intercession at the plague: the sheep are innocent. First principle: the shepherd-king pleads substitution — the angel with the drawn sword stops at the threshingfloor where mercy meets the offering.
```

**AFTER**

```text
David spoke to God at the plague: the sheep are innocent. First principle: David asked God to punish him instead of his people. God's angel stood over Jerusalem with a sword in his hand ready to strike. The plague stopped at the threshingfloor, where David made his offering.
```

**Why:** the compressed "David's intercession at the plague" now names the actor and the act, and the em-dash pile "the shepherd-king pleads substitution — the angel with the drawn sword stops at the threshingfloor where mercy meets the offering" is split so every sentence carries one idea. The source's own unquoted compression "the angel with the drawn sword" is left unquoted and written out in plain words (God's angel, a sword in his hand, ready to strike), which is what 1 Chr 21:16 shows; no quotation marks are added because the source's five words are not the canon's wording ("having a drawn sword in his hand"). Claims kept: David speaks at the plague, the sheep are innocent, the shepherd-king pleads, the angel stops, the place is the threshingfloor, and mercy meets the offering there.

### `1ch-17-11` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
The Nathan-prophecy's seed raised after David, kingdom established. First principle: Chronicles re-preaches 2 Samuel 7 to the returned exiles — the throne-promise was still alive after the fall.
```

**AFTER**

```text
God gave Nathan the prophet a promise about David's son. God would raise up that son after David and establish his kingdom. First principle: Chronicles repeats 2 Samuel 7 to the people who came back from exile. The promise of a lasting throne was still alive after the fall of Jerusalem.
```

**Why:** the label pile "The Nathan-prophecy's seed raised after David, kingdom established" had no subject or verb, so the sentence now names God as the one who gives the promise and Nathan as the prophet who carries it, and the two promises become their own sentence. "re-preaches" becomes "repeats", which everyone can read. Claims kept: the promise comes through Nathan the prophet, the son is raised after David, the kingdom is established, Chronicles speaks it to the returned exiles, the passage is 2 Samuel 7, and the throne-promise outlived the fall of Jerusalem.

Claims kept: the words stand in David's psalm of thanks to the people, God protects the family bound to him by his promise and those who speak for him, and the patriarchs' testimony is Israel's inheritance.

Claims kept: the announcement is public, the son is chosen, the choosing comes before the work, and the father-son word is the reason the building may go ahead.

Claims kept: mercy is not taken away, Saul is the earlier case, the decree is a father-and-son decree, mercy remains, and Hebrews' comparison of Jesus with the angels rests on this promise.

Claims kept: the assembly praises, the kingdom and the headship above all belong to the LORD, David prays this way over the temple gifts, everything is God's, and the Lord's prayer's kingdom-glory line echoes it.
