## 1 · Rewritten strings (BEFORE → AFTER)

### `lam-1-12` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
The passing crowd summoned to behold. First principle: the daughter of Zion's day-of-anger sorrow is the portrait Jerusalem's greater Son reenacted — a sorrow without equal.
```

**AFTER**

```text
The crowd walking past is called to stop and look. First principle: the daughter of Zion grieves on the day of God's anger. No other sorrow is like hers. Jesus, Jerusalem's greater Son, walked through that same sorrow when He suffered.
```

**Why:** "summoned to behold" and "the portrait ... reenacted" were the words of an essay, and the em-dash joined the picture to its fulfilment in one 20-word line. Now the crowd is called to look, Zion's grief is its own sentence, and the greater Son is named as Jesus and given the verb. "a sorrow without equal" is kept as "No other sorrow is like hers". The daughter of Zion and Jerusalem are unchanged.

### `lam-2-11` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
The weeping prophet's insides poured out for destroyed children. First principle: prophetic grief bodily — Jeremiah's tears over the city prefigure the Man of sorrows weeping over Jerusalem.
```

**AFTER**

```text
The weeping prophet pours out his heart for the children who were destroyed. First principle: his grief shows itself in his body. Jeremiah wept over the city, and the Man of sorrows wept over Jerusalem in the same way.
```

**Why:** "prophetic grief bodily" had no verb and no actor, and "prefigure" is a term the reader must decode. Now the prophet pours himself out, and the second sentence says plainly that the grief showed in his body. "prefigure" became "in the same way", with Jeremiah and the Man of sorrows both named. Jerusalem is unchanged.

### `lam-3-40` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
Turning again to the LORD. First principle: the examined life is the returned life — searching ways precedes the turn; hearts lifted with hands.
```

**AFTER**

```text
They turn back to the LORD. First principle: first we search our ways, and then we turn. We lift our hearts up with our hands.
```

**Why:** "the examined life is the returned life" was a slogan, and "searching ways precedes the turn" reversed the plain order. Now the reader is addressed directly with we, the search comes before the turn in ordinary order, and the lifted hearts and hands are a short sentence of their own. The LORD is unchanged.

### `lam-5-19` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
The throne from generation to generation. First principle: the unchanging throne amid the changing ruins — the lament ends on permanence, not loss.
```

**AFTER**

```text
The throne stands from generation to generation. First principle: God's throne stays the same while everything else falls apart. So the lament ends on what lasts, not on what was lost.
```

**Why:** "amid the changing ruins" and "the lament ends on permanence" were abstract nouns stacked on each other. Now the throne stays the same while things fall apart, and the ending is said in everyday words. The grief is not softened and the claim is not changed.

### `lam-3-22` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
Compassions that fail not; new every morning. First principle: the survival-theology verse — non-consumption is mercy's daily freshness, not Israel's merit.
```

**AFTER**

```text
His compassions fail not; they are new every morning. First principle: we are not consumed because God is merciful. His mercy is fresh every day, and Israel did nothing to earn it.
```

**Why:** "the survival-theology verse" filed the verse under a label instead of saying what it claims, and "non-consumption is mercy's daily freshness, not Israel's merit" carried three abstract nouns in a row. Now the claim is plain: we are not consumed because God is merciful, His mercy is fresh every day, and Israel did nothing to earn it. The KJV compassions and every morning are unchanged, and Israel is still named.
