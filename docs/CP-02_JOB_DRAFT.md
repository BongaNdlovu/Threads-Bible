## 1 · Rewritten strings (BEFORE → AFTER)

### `job-19-25` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
Amid intense agony and abandonment, Job makes the monumental confession: "I know that my Redeemer lives, and He shall stand at the last upon the earth; and after my skin is destroyed, yet in my flesh I shall see God." First principle: bodily resurrection and personal vindication through an eternal Kinsman-Redeemer are anchored in the oldest poetry of Scripture.
```

**AFTER**

```text
Job is in agony, and his friends have all turned away. Still he makes this monumental confession: "I know that my Redeemer lives, and He shall stand at the last upon the earth; and after my skin is destroyed, yet in my flesh I shall see God." First principle: bodily resurrection and personal vindication through an eternal Kinsman-Redeemer are anchored in the oldest poetry of Scripture.
```

**Why:** BEFORE opened with a stacked abstraction ("intense agony and abandonment") and buried the speech inside a participle phrase. AFTER puts Job first, splits the scene into its own short sentence, keeps the quotation byte-identical, and lets the confession stand alone. QUOTE-REVIEW: "and He shall stand at the last upon the earth" vs "and that he shall stand at the latter day upon the earth" (Job 19:25); "and after my skin is destroyed" vs "And though after my skin worms destroy this body" (Job 19:26).

### `job-1-21` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text

```

**AFTER**

```text
Job lost everything, and he still said, "the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away;". First principle: Job came into the world with nothing and would leave with nothing. So he owned that the Giver has the right to take back what he gave, and he would not let sin onto his lips.
```

**Why:** BEFORE was telegraphic ("Naked in, naked out") and abstract ("stewardship of loss", "the Giver's rights are acknowledged at the moment of the taking"). AFTER names Job, says what he lost, quotes the verse word for word in quotation marks — KJV reading checked at Job 1:21, "the LORD gave, and the LORD hath taken away;" — keeps the nakedness of the verse in plain words, and states the same claims about God's right to take back and about Job's lips staying free of sin.
