### `neh-9-17` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
The Levites' confession: hardened necks, yet a pardoning God who forsook not. First principle: the history-chapter of Nehemiah 9 is one long proof that God's readiness to pardon outruns rebellion.
```

**AFTER**

```text
The Levites confessed that the people had hardened their necks against God. First principle: Nehemiah 9 is one long chapter of history, and it proves one thing. God is always more ready to pardon than his people are to rebel.
```

**Why:** V2: the colon chain is gone and the history sentence is split so that each sentence carries one idea. V3: the abstract phrase the history-chapter of Nehemiah 9 becomes one long chapter of history, and God's readiness to pardon outruns rebellion becomes God is always more ready to pardon than his people are to rebel. The KJV clause hardened their necks is carried unquoted with its words unchanged, and the meaning is shown by the added words against God. Every claim is kept: the Levites' confession, the hardened necks, God as a pardoning God who did not forsake them, the history chapter, and pardon outrunning rebellion.

Every claim is kept: the covenant-keeping God, the mercy, Jerusalem's ruins, the start of prayer, greatness, the fear of God, covenant mercy, those who love God, and those who keep his commandments.
