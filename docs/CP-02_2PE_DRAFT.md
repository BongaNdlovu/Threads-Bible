### `2pe-1-19` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
Prophecy is the lamp in the dark place until the Day dawns. First principle: the transfigured Mount certifies the written word — the shining day-star is the goal the lamp serves until morning.
```

**AFTER**

```text
The word of prophecy is the lamp that burns in the dark place until the Day dawns. First principle: the glory on the mount confirms the written word. The day-star is the goal, and the lamp serves until morning comes.
```

**Why:** 'Prophecy is the lamp in the dark place' makes prophecy itself the lamp, when the source means the written word of prophecy; the subject is now named. The source put its structural label in the middle of the string and joined the two ideas with an em-dash; the label 'First principle:' is kept verbatim in its original place and the sentence now ends at a full stop. 'the transfigured Mount certifies the written word' uses 'certifies' and 'transfigured' where everyday words will do, so it becomes 'the glory on the mount confirms the written word'. 'the shining day-star is the goal the lamp serves until morning' packs two ideas into one clause behind a comma; they are now two sentences, and 'the day-star' — the source's own word — is kept with 'morning comes' supplying the meaning. No claim is added or dropped.
