## 1 · Rewritten strings (BEFORE → AFTER)

### `sng-5-16` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
Beloved and friend, altogether lovely. First principle: the superlative without comparison — altogether lovely is the whole description; the Friend of sinners is the Friend here sung.
```

**AFTER**

```text
He is my beloved and my friend. He is altogether lovely. First principle: no one can be praised higher than that. That one phrase, altogether lovely, is the whole description. The Friend of sinners is the friend this song is about.
```

**Why:** Replaces the grammar label 'the superlative without comparison' and the inverted 'the Friend here sung' with plain sentences. 'Altogether lovely' and 'the Friend of sinners' are kept unchanged, and the reading that the Friend of sinners is the one sung here is kept at full strength.

### `sng-2-16` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
Mutual possession among the lilies. First principle: the covenant formula in love-song grammar — He feedeth His flock, and the mutual belonging is the song's center.
```

**AFTER**

```text
My beloved is mine, and I am his. He feeds his flock among the lilies. First principle: these two belong to each other. That is the language of God's binding promise. It is also the center of this song.
```

**Why:** 'The covenant formula in love-song grammar' becomes the glossary wording 'God's binding promise', and 'He feedeth His flock' gets a plain verb. The lilies stay as the concrete image they are, and the mutual belonging is still called the song's center.

### `sng-8-6` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
Love strong as death, jealousy cruel as the grave, coals of vehement flame. First principle: love's intensity is death-and-grave scaled — the seal on heart and arm is exclusive covenant possession.
```

**AFTER**

```text
Love is strong as death. Jealousy is cruel as the grave. Its coals are coals of fire, a blazing flame. First principle: this love is as strong as death and the grave. The seal on the heart and on the arm marks God's binding promise, kept for one person alone.
```

**Why:** 'death-and-grave scaled' and 'exclusive covenant possession' become plain sentences with the glossary wording for covenant, and 'vehement' is replaced by 'blazing'. The heart-and-arm seal, the grave and the coals of fire are all kept as images.

### `sng-5-1` · `principle`

**AFTER**

```text
I have gathered my myrrh with my spice. Friends are invited to drink abundantly. First principle: he comes into his garden. He opens the feast to his friends. This is the supper language of the covenant meal — the meal of God's binding promise.
```

**Why:** The fragment 'The gathered myrrh and spice' and the label 'the garden entered, the feast opened to friends' become full sentences with subjects. 'Covenant meal' is defined in the same sentence with the glossary wording, and the myrrh and spice are kept.

### `sng-8-7` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text

```

**AFTER**

```text

```

**Why:** 'Unquenchable' and 'unpurchasable' are said in everyday words, and 'Revelation's marriage follows the unquenched' becomes a sentence that names Revelation and the marriage without changing the claim.

### `sng-4-10` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text

```

**AFTER**

```text
Your love is better than wine. The smell of your ointments is better than all spices. First principle: this love is the measure of the marriage song. It is also the measure of the Messiah's joy. The Messiah is the promised King, Jesus. His joy is better wine still.
```

**Why:** 'Outsmells all spices' becomes a plain sentence that keeps the spices, and 'the Messiah's own better-wine joy' is unpacked into whole sentences. The Messiah is defined with the glossary wording, and no claim is added beyond the source's.

### `sng-1-3` · `principle`

**BEFORE**


**AFTER**

**Why:** 'His name's fragrance' and 'the love-song's ointment-name anticipates the anointed One' become plain sentences, and 'the anointed One' is defined with the glossary wording for Messianic. Bethany, the nard and the virgins are kept, and the forward-pointing reading is kept exactly as strong as the source has it.
