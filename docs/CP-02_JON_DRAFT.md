## 1 · Rewritten strings (BEFORE → AFTER)

### `jon-1-17` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
Now the LORD had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. A divinely appointed descent and rescue — Jonah himself prays it: thou hadst cast me into the deep... yet hast thou brought up my life from corruption. First principle: God signs the resurrection with a type before He performs it in His Son.
```

**AFTER**

```text
Now the LORD had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. God appointed both the descent into the fish and the rescue out of it. Jonah himself prays about it: "thou hadst cast me into the deep... yet hast thou brought up my life from corruption." First principle: God gave the resurrection a sign before He performed it. The earlier picture was Jonah. His Son was the fulfillment.
```

**Why:** 'A divinely appointed descent and rescue' is a noun stack with no actor, and the em-dash plus the colon hold three ideas in one sentence. God is now named as the one who appointed both the descent into the fish and the rescue out of it, and each idea has its own sentence. The quoted line is the source's own Jonah 2:3 and 2:6 wording: quotation marks were added around it with every word, and the source's own ellipsis, unchanged. The two KJV sentences at the front ('Now the LORD had prepared a great fish to swallow up Jonah.' and 'And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.') are carried byte-identical. The frozen glossary rendering for type is used: the earlier picture. No claim added or dropped.

### `jon-2-1` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text

```

**AFTER**

```text
Jonah prayed from inside the fish. Nobody would call that an address for prayer. First principle: the belly of the fish was a temple. The prophet prayed toward the holy place from the deep. God heard him.
```

**Why:** 'Prayer from the impossible address' is a verbless fragment the reader must decode, and the em-dash sentence ends in the passive 'and is heard'. It is now four plain statements with the actors named: Jonah prayed, the belly was a temple, the prophet prayed toward the holy place from the deep, and God heard him. Every claim survives and the 'First principle:' label is verbatim.

**BEFORE**

**AFTER**


**Why:** 'God's character-statement' becomes God's own description of Himself, and the colon plus the em-dash sentence is split so each idea stands alone. Exodus 34, the runaway's reason and the missionary's problem are all kept.

### `jon-4-11` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
Sixscore thousand who cannot discern their right hand — plus cattle. First principle: the book ends on God's pity-question; compassion scales to the ignorant and even to the animals.
```

**AFTER**

```text
The city held Sixscore thousand people who cannot discern their right hand. It held much cattle too. First principle: the book ends on God's pity-question. God's compassion reaches people who do not know better. It reaches even the animals.
```

**Why:** The em-dash fragment becomes two sentences with a subject and verb, and the abstract 'compassion scales to the ignorant' is written as who is actually reached. QUOTE-REVIEW: the unquoted 'Sixscore thousand who cannot discern their right hand' compresses Jonah 4:11 (KJV: 'more than sixscore thousand persons that cannot discern between their right hand and their left hand'). Every word of that compression is carried byte-identical and it stays unquoted; only the word 'people' was added so the sentence has a subject. The pity-question and the cattle are kept.
