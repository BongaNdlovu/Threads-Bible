```text
OT Sheol; conquered by the risen Lord.
```

**AFTER**

```text
This is Sheol in the OT. The risen Lord conquered it.
```

**Why:** V2: the semicolon clause had no subject and no verb for its first half (OT Sheol; conquered by the risen Lord), so the reader had to supply both; it is now two sentences with a subject and a verb each. V3: the passive conquered by the risen Lord becomes active with the risen Lord as the actor. Sheol is untouched, and the abbreviation OT is kept byte-identical rather than expanded to Old Testament.

### `rev-7-2` · `terms[0].gloss`

**BEFORE**

```text
seal, stamp of ownership and authentication
```

**AFTER**

```text
seal, a stamp that shows who owns it and that it is genuine
```

**Why:** V3: authentication is the one word in this gloss a reader must decode, and the gloss exists to explain the term; it now says who owns the thing and that it is genuine. V2: the two bare noun phrases (stamp of ownership and authentication) become one clause with a verb. The term seal and the sibling fields holding the Greek σφραγῖδα, the transliteration sphragida and the note are untouched.
