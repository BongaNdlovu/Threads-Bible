## 1 · Rewritten strings (BEFORE → AFTER)

### `phm-1-9` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
Love's appeal from an old prisoner. First principle: authority is laid down for love's sake — the apostle beseeches rather than commands, and the prisoner's chains are his credential.
```

**AFTER**

```text
Paul appeals out of love. He writes as an old prisoner. First principle: for love's sake he sets his authority aside. The apostle pleads rather than commands, and his chains as a prisoner are the claim he brings.
```

**Why:** “Love's appeal from an old prisoner” is a noun phrase, so it becomes two sentences with Paul as the actor. The passive abstraction “authority is laid down for love's sake” becomes “for love's sake he sets his authority aside”, “beseeches” becomes the everyday “pleads”, and the metaphor “the prisoner's chains are his credential” becomes “his chains as a prisoner are the claim he brings”. Every claim is kept: the apostle asks instead of commanding, and his chains are what he offers.

### `phm-1-25` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text
The grace-benediction closes the shortest letter. First principle: grace with the spirit is the lasting need — every wrong righted begins and ends in grace to the inner man.
```

**AFTER**

```text
The shortest letter closes with a blessing of grace. First principle: what the spirit needs most is grace. Every wrong that is put right begins and ends with grace for the inner person.
```

**Why:** The coined compound “grace-benediction” becomes “a blessing of grace”, and “grace with the spirit is the lasting need” is written as “what the spirit needs most is grace” — the same claim in plain word order. The em-dash chain is split, and “every wrong righted begins and ends in grace to the inner man” becomes “Every wrong that is put right begins and ends with grace for the inner person”, which keeps “begins and ends in grace” and replaces the dated “inner man” with “inner person”.

### `phm-1-6` · `principle`

**BEFORE**

```text

```

**AFTER**

```text
Your faith becomes effective when you acknowledge every good thing that is in us in Christ. First principle: faith is shared when a person names what is in Christ. That is what gives the sharing its power.
```

**Why:** “Effective faith acknowledges every good thing in us in Christ” becomes a second-person sentence about the reader's own faith, with “becomes effective when” supplying the plain word order the source's KJV phrasing implies. “faith communicates by acknowledging” becomes “faith is shared when a person names what is in Christ”, and the jargon verb “energizes” becomes “gives the sharing its power”. The two claims — acknowledgement, and the sharing it empowers — are both kept.


**Why:** The source's fragment “Perhaps — that thou shouldest receive him for ever” becomes a sentence, with “he departed for a season” supplied from the entry's own title (“He Therefore Departed for a Season”); the clause “thou shouldest receive him for ever” is carried word for word and left unquoted. “providence reads departures as returns” becomes “God looks at a departure and sees a return” — the actor named, the image kept — and the coined “forever-reception reframes the season of loss” becomes “Receiving him for ever changes how you see the season you lost”.
